Fast furniture (think particleboard shelving designed to survive one move)
is high-volume, short-lived, and rarely recyclable. It's also one of the
most tractable categories for open, distributed manufacture: CNC-cut
plywood, standard fasteners, no exotic tooling required.

**Why it scores well:** huge existing waste volume, very feasible to design
and build in a local makerspace or small shop, large pool of makers already
active in this space to draw on.

**By the numbers:** the global furniture market was worth roughly $667
billion in 2024, and unit sales in single sub-categories are enormous on
their own (around 35 million sofas a year in the US market alone). There's
no single reliable global "units of furniture sold" figure, but industry
commentary consistently describes "fast furniture" — cheap, mass-produced,
particleboard-based — as designed for a handful of years at most, well
short of the 7–15+ years quality furniture is expected to last.
