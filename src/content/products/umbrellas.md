An umbrella is mechanically simple — fabric canopy, a frame of ribs and a
shaft — yet the cheap end of the market is engineered for a short life:
thin stamped-metal ribs that bend in the first strong gust, a canopy bonded
to the frame in a way that makes repair harder than replacement. Once one
rib goes, most people don't fix it, they bin it and buy another.

**Why it scores well:** the failure mode is almost always one small part
(a rib joint, a spring), which makes this a strong repairability case —
a design built around replaceable ribs and a separable canopy could turn a
disposable object into a genuinely durable one, using simple, widely
available materials (fiberglass or steel rod, ripstop fabric).

**By the numbers:** the number "1 billion+ umbrellas discarded per year
worldwide" shows up constantly in articles about umbrella waste, but as far
as we can tell nobody has traced it to an original source — we're including
it as a widely repeated estimate, not a verified fact, in the spirit of not
hiding uncertainty. What's better documented at the individual-product
level: budget umbrellas are frequently reported to last only a few months
to a year, while well-built, repairable umbrellas can last 10–25 years —
a roughly 20x difference in lifespan for a broadly similar object.
