Most cookware today bonds dissimilar metals and coatings together, so it's
thrown out the moment the coating fails. A mono-material, refinishable
design (cast iron, plain stainless) with swappable handles could last a
lifetime.

**Why it scores well:** high replacement frequency today, moderate
feasibility (metalworking is more specialized than woodworking, but well
within reach of existing maker/blacksmith communities).

**By the numbers:** we couldn't find a credible global "units sold per
year" figure for cookware specifically — if you have one, [open an
issue](https://github.com/3IVIS/openecogoods/issues/new?template=propose-product.md).
What's well documented is the lifespan problem: most non-stick pans last
1–2 years before the coating degrades, and even premium ceramic coatings
are usually rated for only a few years of daily use before replacement.
